# Which office number fits which ask
Researched: 2026-11-10 · Informs: [call-the-right-office](../prd/archive/2026-11-call-the-right-office.md)
Supersedes: action-conversion-levers.md (only the verdict "show the district number first when the DC line is likely closed")

## Question
A member of Congress has a DC office and one or more district offices. For
each kind of ask the site makes (a vote on a bill, a party leadership vote,
a state treasurer's decision), which number should the caller dial?

## Evidence
- **Field report, 2026-11-10.** A constituent called Rep. Lou Correa's
  district office to ask him to back a new House Democratic leader. Staff
  said only the DC office handles that and they could not pass it along.
  One call, but it is the exact path the act page sends a Pacific caller
  down after 15:00 local, when DC is closed and the district number is
  listed first.
- **Votes and timely issues → DC.** Union of Concerned Scientists, "How to
  Have a Productive Phone Call With Your Legislator's Office"
  (https://www.ucs.org/resources/how-have-productive-phone-call-your-legislators-office):
  "If you are calling about a vote or other timely issues, always call the
  Washington office." The local office suits general issues affecting the
  district.
- **Policy → the DC legislative assistant.** ANCOR advocacy toolkit
  (https://www.ancor.org/advocacy/toolkit/contacting-members-of-congress):
  for support or opposition on an issue, contact the legislative assistant
  in Washington who handles it. Caseworkers in the state or district
  offices handle problems with federal agencies.
- **District office = constituent services.** AAPM&R "Basics of Calling
  Congress" (https://www.physiatry.org/page/congress_telephone): the
  district office staff handle constituent services and may be less
  influential on legislation than the DC staff. CRS, "Casework in
  Congressional Offices: FAQ" (R44696,
  https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20161122_R44696_a553721d67d583e612e539ad8228f672546887fb.html):
  most casework is done in state or district offices; practice varies by
  office.
- **Leadership votes.** Leaders are elected in closed party caucuses
  shortly after the November election; only the Speaker gets a floor vote
  (Ballotpedia, https://ballotpedia.org/U.S._House_leadership_elections,_2025).
  These are political, not legislative, matters. No source says a district
  office logs them, and the field report says one does not. Routing to the
  DC office (chief of staff or the scheduler's front desk) is the only path
  with evidence.
- **Voicemail counts.** `action-conversion-levers.md` and
  `leader-call-scripts.md`: offices tally voicemail daily; leave a name and
  street address. So a closed DC office is still a valid target, which
  removes the reason to send after-hours callers to the district office.
- **Not verified.** The "one CMS, one tally" quote in `email-vs-call.md`
  §A.2 suggests both offices *can* log a position. That is capability, not
  practice; the field report shows practice varies.

## Answer per ask
| Ask (goal category) | Right number | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Vote on a bill (`federal_vote`: ndaa-1217, ndaa-report-vote) | DC office | Votes and timely issues go to the DC legislative staff |
| Party leadership vote (`leadership`: house-caucus, senate-leader) | DC office | Political, decided in DC; a district office refused it |
| State treasurer (`state`: state-bonds) | The treasurer's office line (already the only number) | One decider, one office |
| Local matter or agency problem (none today) | District office | Casework and local issues live there |

## What changed from action-conversion-levers.md
That file adapted "show the district number first when the DC line is likely
closed" so Pacific callers reach a live person. For vote and leadership asks
a live person in the wrong office is worse than a DC voicemail that is
tallied. The other verdicts in that file stand.
